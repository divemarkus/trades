## Federal Tax Credit

The good news is that **battery-only systems qualify**.

The IRS allows a **30% Residential Clean Energy Credit** for residential battery storage installed after 2022, even if **there are no solar panels**. The battery must:

- be **new**
- have at least **3 kWh** of storage
- be **installed in connection with your home**
- be placed into service during the tax year claimed. citeturn0search3turn0search1

The IRS also confirms that standalone battery storage is eligible under the Residential Clean Energy Credit. citeturn0search0turn0search5

### Does Pila qualify?

This is where things become a little nuanced.

The current Pila Mesh Battery is advertised as a **plug-in residential battery** rather than a permanently hardwired battery.

The IRS language says:

> "installed in connection with your home."

There is **no explicit requirement that a licensed electrician install it**, but the IRS has not published guidance specifically addressing plug-in batteries like Pila. The credit clearly applies to permanently installed home battery systems; whether a portable or plug-in system qualifies depends on whether it is considered "installed" under IRS rules. citeturn0search3turn0search5

I would therefore rate the likelihood as:

| Scenario | Likelihood of 30% credit |
|----------|-------------------------:|
| Permanently installed battery | ★★★★★ Very high |
| Plug-in battery with dedicated installation | ★★★★☆ Likely |
| Portable battery sitting on floor | ★★☆☆☆ Questionable |

So if Pila eventually offers (or recommends) a fixed installation kit, I'd feel much more comfortable claiming the credit.

## Washington State

There is currently **no statewide homeowner tax credit** specifically for buying residential batteries.

However, Washington has been moving toward battery incentives.

A state law created a residential battery incentive program that utilities can participate in beginning in mid-2026. Large electric utilities are expected to offer incentives for qualifying grid-connected batteries through programs administered with the Washington State University Extension Energy Program. The exact incentive amounts depend on your utility and program rules. citeturn0search4turn0search12

If you're served by utilities such as:
- Seattle City Light
- Snohomish PUD
- Puget Sound Energy (PSE)
- Tacoma Power

it's worth checking whether they have launched their battery incentive or virtual power plant program.

Since you've discussed building out your home infrastructure incrementally rather than making one large capital purchase, I'd lean toward this approach:

1. Buy **1–2 Pila units** to cover essentials (network gear, refrigerator, lighting, internet).
