# Bitcoin

> If the CLARITY Act passes and we proceed with this scenario. Is it safe to say "Every asset class will continue to depreciate when measured against Bitcoin."

No.

It's a plausible thesis, but it is **not safe to state it as a fact**.

A more defensible statement would be:

> **Bitcoin has a reasonable probability of outperforming most major asset classes over long periods if global liquidity expands, institutional adoption continues, and its monetary properties remain intact.**

That's very different from saying:

> **Every asset class will continue to depreciate against Bitcoin.**

---

# The Bitcoin Maximalist Thesis

The argument goes like this:

```text
Fiat Supply
     ↑

Asset Inflation
     ↑

Bitcoin Supply
     Fixed

Result:
Everything trends toward Bitcoin
```

Michael Saylor and many Bitcoin advocates essentially believe:

> Bitcoin is the ultimate monetary asset.

If true:

* Stocks depreciate against Bitcoin.
* Real estate depreciates against Bitcoin.
* Gold depreciates against Bitcoin.
* Bonds depreciate against Bitcoin.
* Fiat depreciates against Bitcoin.

Historically, over the past 15 years, that has largely been true.

---

# The Problem With Extrapolation

Many investors make this mistake:

```text
2011-2026

Bitcoin Outperformed

↓

Therefore

2026-2050

Bitcoin Must Continue Outperforming
```

History doesn't guarantee future returns.

---

# Consider Gold

Gold has survived:

* Roman Empire
* Byzantine Empire
* British Empire
* Bretton Woods
* Nixon Shock

for thousands of years.

Bitcoin has survived 17 years.

That's impressive.

But they're not equivalent track records.

---

# The Bigger Bitcoin Question

The question isn't:

> Is Bitcoin better than dollars?

The question is:

> Can Bitcoin absorb enough global wealth to justify future returns?

# Where The CLARITY Act Fits

The CLARITY Act doesn't automatically make Bitcoin the winner.

It primarily:

* Clarifies regulation
* Encourages U.S. crypto innovation
* Supports digital asset infrastructure
* Helps stablecoin adoption

In fact, one could argue:

```text
CLARITY
    ↓
Stablecoins
    ↓
Treasury Demand
    ↓
Dollar Strength
```

which could strengthen the dollar system rather than weaken it.

Consider:


But it would no longer outperform everything.

Or:

```text

Becomes Mature
```

# The Key Distinction

There is a huge difference between:

> "Bitcoin is the best-performing asset."

and

> "Every asset will depreciate against Bitcoin."

The first is plausible.

The second assumes Bitcoin becomes the dominant global store of value and continues absorbing capital from virtually every competing asset class for decades.

That could happen, but it is far from certain.

The strongest version of the Bitcoin bull thesis is not that Bitcoin replaces everything.

It's that Bitcoin becomes a permanent global monetary asset alongside:

* Gold
* Treasuries
* Equities
* Real estate

and captures a significantly larger share of global wealth than it does today.
